ATI Flow architecture

A mental model for understanding how factory demand becomes coordinated robot work.

The system at a glance

Layer 1

Business layer

The factory creates the reason for movement. Examples include production demand, material availability, line schedules or inventory conditions.

Layer 2

Integration layer

Integrations connect external systems to ATI Flow. The deployment material describes ERP hooks, status/completion updates, priority logic and master-data alignment here.

Layer 3

Orchestration layer

ATI Flow translates configured workflows and incoming requests into executable work. Maps, workflows, fleets, robots and operational monitoring meet here.

Layer 4

Fleet layer

Fleet management coordinates multiple robots: task allocation, shared-resource arbitration, charging/idle behaviour and operational priorities.

Layer 5

Robot / autonomy layer

The robot executes movement using its own navigation, perception, localization, safety and drive systems.

Design implication: expose the operational decision a user needs rather than reproducing every internal robot state. Low-level diagnostics can remain in Debug.
